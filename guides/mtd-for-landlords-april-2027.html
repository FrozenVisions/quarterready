Making Tax Digital for landlords: April 2027

Short answer: if your qualifying income from property (and any self-employment) was over £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year, you must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2027 (GOV.UK).

What counts as qualifying income

Qualifying income is your total gross income from self-employment and property in a tax year, before expenses (GOV.UK). So a landlord with £32,000 rent and big costs still crosses the £30,000 line.

How many landlords are affected

HMRC's figures (based on 2023 to 2024 returns) estimate 182,000 landlords-only, plus 97,000 people with both property and self-employment income, in the £30,000–£50,000 band joining from April 2027 (GOV.UK statistics).

What you'll do each quarter

  • Keep digital records of rent and expenses.
  • Send a quarterly update through compatible software. Deadlines are 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May (GOV.UK). See our deadlines guide.
  • Submit your tax return using software after the year ends.

Jointly let property

For jointly let properties you can include either income and expenses, or income only, in your quarterly updates. If you leave expenses out, you report them by resending your fourth quarterly update before your tax return (GOV.UK).

Next steps

  1. Check your 2025-26 qualifying income.
  2. Sign up early on GOV.UK. HMRC is urging this (GOV.UK).
  3. Choose compatible software, including bridging software if you want to keep a spreadsheet.
